FAQ

Frequently asked questions

General, educational answers about how the referral link works and what the early stages of a mortgage conversation usually involve. Nothing here is specific to any person, property or loan.

Read this first

Meadowbrook Mortgage Demo is a fictional business built as a portfolio demonstration by 1MR LLC. Nothing below is an offer to lend, a commitment to lend, or financial advice. For guidance about your own situation, speak to a licensed mortgage professional.

Is this a real mortgage company?

No. Meadowbrook Mortgage Demo is fictional. It exists to demonstrate a static site build, URL-based personalization and CRM form integration. The loan officers, testimonials, address and phone number are invented, and no form on this site transmits anything anywhere.

My agent sent me a link. Why did it have my agent's name in it?

Referral links carry two pieces of context in the address itself: who referred you, and which loan officer is expected to look after the enquiry. The landing page reads both straight out of the link, which is why it can greet you by your agent's name without you filling anything in.

You can see it working on the demo landing page and change the values in the address bar yourself.

What happens if the link is mistyped or forwarded?

Nothing breaks. If the referral details are missing, misspelled or point at somebody who is not on the team, the page falls back to a generic welcome and carries on. You will not see an error message, and you can still get in touch the normal way.

What does pre-qualification actually mean?

In general industry usage, pre-qualification is an early, informal conversation. You describe your circumstances, a loan officer explains the options that tend to fit situations like that, and you both get a sense of whether it is worth going further.

It is not an approval and it is not a commitment to lend. Anything discussed at that stage remains subject to a complete application, documentation, underwriting and credit approval.

How is pre-qualification different from pre-approval?

The terms are used loosely across the industry, and different lenders define them differently, so it is always worth asking what a particular lender means by them. Broadly, pre-qualification is the lighter, conversational step, while pre-approval involves submitting documentation and a review by the lender. Neither one is a guarantee of financing.

What documents are usually involved?

This varies by lender, by loan type and by your circumstances, and the list you are given may look nothing like the one below. As a general matter, lenders commonly ask about:

  • Proof of identity
  • Recent income documentation, such as pay statements or tax returns
  • Recent bank or asset statements
  • Details of current housing costs
  • Information about existing debts and obligations

Self-employed applicants are typically asked for a longer history than salaried applicants. Ask for the specific list before you start gathering anything.

How long does the process take?

There is no meaningful general answer, and anyone who gives you a firm number before seeing your file is guessing. Timing depends on the loan type, how quickly documentation arrives, the property itself, and factors outside the lender's control. A good loan officer will tell you what the next step is and what is likely to hold it up.

Does it cost anything to ask a question?

Initial conversations with a loan officer are ordinarily free and carry no obligation. Costs associated with an actual mortgage application, such as third-party fees, are disclosed to you in writing before you commit to anything. Ask for that in writing, always.

What happens to the information I submit?

On a real site, an application form of this kind would pass your details to the branch's CRM, along with the referring agent and the loan officer who owns the enquiry, and would be handled under that firm's privacy policy.

On this site nothing is transmitted at all. The form on the apply page is a non-functional mock with no destination. Please do not enter real personal or financial information anywhere on this demo. See the sample privacy policy for the fuller picture.

Can I speak to someone instead of filling in a form?

On a real branch site, yes — by phone, by text, by email or by booking a call. Because this business is fictional, none of those routes reaches a person here, but the booking page shows how the scheduling step would fit into the flow.
