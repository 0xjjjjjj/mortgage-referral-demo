From Accepted Offer to Closing Day: What Actually Happens in Between

July 22, 2026 · 2 min read

An offer gets accepted. Champagne, maybe. Then a strange quiet sets in, and a buyer is left wondering what's supposed to happen next. Here's roughly how that stretch of time tends to unfold.

The first two weeks

Inspections typically get scheduled almost immediately, since any requested repairs or price adjustments need time to be negotiated before other deadlines start pressing. A full loan application, if one hasn't already been submitted, usually happens right around now too.

The appraisal enters the picture

A lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home is worth roughly what's being paid for it. This step is often the one buyers worry about most, since a low appraisal can send everyone back to the negotiating table. Underwriting is usually happening in parallel, reviewing the buyer's documentation in full.

The home stretch

Once underwriting issues a final approval, a closing date gets locked in. A final walkthrough happens shortly before, and then, the paperwork signing itself, which is usually far more routine than the weeks leading up to it.

A note on this article

This post is generic educational content written for the Meadowbrook Mortgage Demo portfolio project. It is not financial advice, and actual timelines vary by transaction, lender and local requirements.
