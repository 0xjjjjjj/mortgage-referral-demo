The Paperwork Checklist: What Lenders Typically Ask Buyers to Gather

May 14, 2026 · 2 min read

Nobody enjoys hunting down a bank statement from three months ago. But most of the documents a lender asks for exist for a specific reason, and knowing that reason ahead of time makes the request feel a lot less arbitrary. Here's a rundown of what tends to come up early.

Proof of income

Recent pay stubs, W-2s, and often a couple of years of tax returns. Lenders typically look at these to understand how steady and how large an income is, since a mortgage is a long-term commitment built around it.

Proof of assets

Bank and investment account statements show where a down payment and closing costs are coming from. Large, unexplained deposits often prompt a follow-up question, simply because an underwriter needs to know the source of every dollar involved.

The smaller items that add up

Identification, an explanation of any employment gaps, documentation for other income like alimony or a side business. None of these are unusual to be asked for; they're simply part of building a complete picture of a buyer's finances.
