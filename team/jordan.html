About Jordan

Jordan's files rarely look like a standard W-2 application, and that's the point. Self-employed borrowers, 1099 contractors and small business owners tend to have income that's real but not simple, spread across schedules, K-1s and write-offs that lower a tax bill but complicate a debt-to-income calculation. Jordan's job is to find the version of that income a lender can actually work with, and to explain, in plain terms, why the paperwork list is longer than a typical buyer's.

Investment property is the other lane, and it comes with its own vocabulary: rental income offsets, reserve requirements, and financing structures that behave differently once a property stops being a primary residence. Jordan treats every investor conversation as a chance to map out the next few purchases, not just the one in front of them.

Agent partners who work with investors and small business owners tend to gravitate toward Jordan specifically because these files take patience, and Jordan doesn't rush them. A referral that says "self-employed, complicated income" is treated as a normal Tuesday, not a red flag.

If Jordan is your referral, expect a longer document checklist up front, a clear explanation of why each item matters, and someone who has seen the income structure you're worried about before.
